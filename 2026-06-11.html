Science and engineering

One of my recent concern is my major. Though I already know that I want to become a ‘leader in pharmaceutical and biotech field’, I also found out that there are numerous ways to achieve that. I can major in chemical engineering, graduate MS in the same field, get MBA, and continue my career not as the R&D scientist or engineer but as the pharmacy firm analyst or VC manager. (Many CEOs like John Rim chose this path.) Else, I can major in chemistry (or chemical engineering), graduate Phd in chemical engineering, and work as a scientist or an engineer in the drug discovery field or biotech. I prefer the latter as I have deep interest in the science and engineering. I also found out that what I consequently want is the engineering: I do not just want to find the mechanism of some chemical reaction or drug intake but to create the system of the drug or treatment that can save millions of people.

However, in the field of pharmacy and biotech, science and engineering are both needed for the successful novel drug and system. Creating the product itself without knowing the exact mechanism is crucial, but I also have to understand the foundational knowledge in chemistry, biology, and physics for the complete research and its application. Talking with the professor in the SNU lab which I recently got in, I got the stronger certainty that the undergraduate study must be focused on the conventional foundation subjects. For example, to become the leader in creating or developing the program for computational analysis in molecular dynamics or protein structure, I have to understand advanced organic chemistry, molecular and cell biology, linear algebra, machine learning, and quantum chemistry with the following wet and dry lab experiences. All those R&D-related basic foundations must be understood before I continue my deeper research in Phd in chemical engineering.

Therefore, I am currently considering my undergraduate study to work on chemistry (specifically, biochemistry with computational chemistry skills) and graduate on BA. (Of course, this path could be changed anytime when I think some other major will be a better choice.) What I eventually want is engineering; now is the time for the strong, sturdy base for the upcoming researches. However, though I may study in the natural science field, I must not forget to have the ‘engineering mindset’. Not to be immersed in the finding itself, but to research things that are directly connected to real-time drug system.

— Jonas Choi
